How to make a home inventory for insurance

After a fire, flood or burglary, your insurer will ask you to list everything you lost, with what it was worth. Very few people can do that from memory. A home inventory made now turns a months-long argument into a short claim.

Why it matters

  • You get paid for what you actually owned. Without a list, people forget most of what was in closets, drawers and the garage, and those are exactly the things that add up.
  • You choose the right amount of cover. Adding up your belongings often shows that a policy's contents limit is too low.
  • Claims go faster. Photos and a list with values answer the adjuster's first questions before they ask them.

What to record for each item

  • What it is, plus the brand and model if it has one
  • How many you have
  • Where it is: the room, and the closet, shelf or box inside it
  • Roughly when you bought it and what it would cost to replace today
  • A photo, and for valuable things the serial number and the receipt

Replacement cost (what it costs to buy new today) and actual cash value (what it was worth used) are different numbers. Check which one your policy pays, and record both if you can.

How to do it in one afternoon

  1. Start with a walk-through. Stand in each doorway and take one wide photo or a short video of the whole room. That alone already proves a lot.
  2. Then go shelf by shelf. Open closets, drawers, cabinets and storage boxes and photograph what's inside. Don't forget the garage, attic, basement, shed and storage unit.
  3. Turn photos into a list. This is the slow part by hand. An app that reads the photo for you saves hours (see below).
  4. Add proof for the expensive things. Electronics, jewelry, bikes, tools, instruments, art and collectibles: serial numbers, receipts, appraisals.
  5. Keep it somewhere other than your house. A list stored only in a desk drawer burns with the desk. Keep it in the cloud or with someone you trust.

Keep it current

Add big purchases when you make them, and walk through the house once a year. Moving, renovating and decluttering are natural moments to update it too.
